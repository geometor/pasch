People¶

Geometers, Logicians & Philosophers of the Axiomatic and Projective Tradition

Primary Author¶

Moritz Pasch (1843–1930)

Professor of Mathematics at the University of Giessen. In Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie (1882), he established the first rigorous axiomatization of linear betweenness and planar triangle separation (Pasch's Axiom), constructed projective ideal elements strictly from finite sheaves (Bündel) without appeal to infinity, and articulated the modern hypothetico-deductive criterion of formal deduction (§ 12, p. 98).

Cited Geometers & Axiomatic Pioneers¶

Karl Georg Christian von Staudt (1798–1867)

Professor of Mathematics at Erlangen. In Geometrie der Lage (1847) and Beiträge zur Geometrie der Lage (1856–1860), he emancipated projective geometry from metrical length and numerical cross-ratios, defining harmonic tetrads, projectivities, conics, and projective coordinates purely through incidence and separation.

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BCE)

Author of the Elements, whose tacit reliance on diagram-based linear betweenness and planar crossbar intersections (notably in I.16 and I.21) was first diagnosed and deductively closed by Moritz Pasch.

Pappus of Alexandria (c. 290–c. 350 CE)

Author of the Synagoge, Book VII of which preserved the invariance of collinear four-point cross-ratios and the Hexagon Theorem (VII.139).

Girard Desargues (1591–1661)

French architect and projective pioneer (Brouillon project, 1639), whose Two-Triangle Theorem guarantees the invariance of complete-quadrangle harmonic fourths and projective arithmetic.

Blaise Pascal (1623–1662)

Discoverer of the Hexagrammum Mysticum (1640) on hexagons inscribed in a conic.

Charles Julien Brianchon (1783–1864)

Formulator of Brianchon’s Theorem (1806) on hexagons circumscribed about a conic, paired with Pascal’s Theorem through projective reciprocity.

Jean-Victor Poncelet (1788–1867)

Author of the Traité des propriétés projectives des figures (1822), who revived synthetic projective geometry and pole–polar duality.

August Ferdinand Möbius (1790–1868)

Author of Der barycentrische Calcul (1827), originator of barycentric coordinates and the rational projective net (Möbiussches Netz).

Jakob Steiner (1796–1863)

Swiss geometer at Berlin (1832, 1833) who generated conics from projective pencils and proved straightedge-only parallel constructions.

Michel Chasles (1793–1880)

French geometer who codified the signed metrical cross-ratio (rapport anharmonique).

Felix Klein (1849–1925)

Author of the Erlangen Program and memoirs on non-Euclidean geometry (1871, 1873), who identified the continuity gap in von Staudt’s Fundamental Theorem that directly motivated Pasch’s order axiomatics.

Jean-Gaston Darboux (1842–1917)

French analyst and geometer who proved (1880) that over $\mathbb{R}$ every harmonic-preserving bijection is continuous and projective.

David Hilbert (1862–1943)

Author of Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899), who adopted Pasch’s planar separation postulate verbatim as Axiom II 4 and adapted von Staudt’s algebra of throws into his Streckenrechnung.

Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)

British philosopher and logician whose Essay on the Foundations of Geometry (1897) and Principles of Mathematics (1903) critically examined Pasch’s descriptive order axioms.

Giovanni Vailati (1863–1909)

Italian logician of the Peano school (1895) who axiomatized cyclical separation of point pairs on closed projective lines in continuation of Pasch’s order axioms.

Oswald Veblen (1880–1960)

American geometer who with J. W. Young (1910) recast von Staudt’s and Pasch’s axiomatics over general fields.

Peter Hubert Jacob Hoenen, S.J. (1880–1961)

Author of De Noetica Geometriae (1954), analyzing Pasch’s Stammsätze and formalist deduction from the standpoint of Aristotelian-Thomistic epistemology.

Alfred Tarski (1901–1983)

Polish-American logician (1959) whose first-order elementary geometry distills planar order into the inner/outer five-point Pasch axiom.
