Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie — Moritz Pasch (Leipzig, 1882)

Author: Moritz Pasch
First German Edition (1882): Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie, Leipzig: Verlag von B. G. Teubner, 1882.

“Indeed, if geometry is to be genuinely deductive, the process of inference must everywhere be independent of the meaning of the geometrical concepts, just as it must be independent of the diagrams; only the relations among the geometrical concepts laid down in the propositions and definitions employed may be taken into account.”

—Moritz Pasch, Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie (1882), § 12, p. 98

§ 1 Critical Notes: From Pasch’s Eight Grundsätze to Hilbert Group II

Direct Lineage to Hilbert’s Axioms II 1–3 (1899): Seventeen years after Pasch’s Vorlesungen, David Hilbert (Grundlagen § 3, geometor/hilbert) compressed Pasch’s Grundsätze I–VIII into his Group II linear order axioms (II 1: symmetry \(A\text{--}B\text{--}C \implies C\text{--}B\text{--}A\) and collinearity; II 2: density + prolongation \(A\text{--}C\text{--}B\) and \(A\text{--}B\text{--}D\); II 3: at most one of three collinear points lies between the other two), noting in 1902 that E. H. Moore proved Hilbert’s original four-point Theorem 4 redundant once Pasch’s planar Axiom II 4 is assumed!

§ 1 Summary: Bounded Segments & Linear Betweenness Axioms

Bounded Segments Before Infinite Lines (*Einleitung*): Pasch begins from finite observable straight segments \(AB\) and the ternary relation “\(C\) lies inside \(AB\)” (betweenness) rather than assuming completed infinite lines a priori.

Pasch’s Planar Separation Axiom (§ 2, *IV. Grundsatz*, p. 21): Any coplanar line avoiding vertices \(A, B, C\) that enters open side \(AB\) must exit through open side \(AC\) or open side \(BC\).
